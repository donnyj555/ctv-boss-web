# "Couple" — :30 CTV Commercial

**Spot ID:** `couple-30`
**Length:** 30.0s exactly (CTV/OTT hard clock)
**Format:** 16:9, 1920×1080, 30fps, stereo, −24 LKFS
**Audience:** Empty-nester / move-up-in-reverse homeowners, 55–74, 15+ years in home
**Goal:** Seller lead. Get a homeowner who has never considered selling to request a home value.

---

## Concept

The house did its job. The kids are gone and the square footage isn't.
We never say "downsize" — that word tests badly with this audience. We show
the cost of a house that's bigger than the life inside it, then offer the
easy first step: find out what it's worth.

No agent on camera, no listing photos, no sold signs. This reads as a story
until the last four seconds, which is why it survives a CTV pod where every
other spot opens with a logo.

## Voiceover

Warm, unhurried, close-mic. Someone their age, not an announcer. ~150 wpm,
which leaves real air between lines — the pauses are doing as much work as
the words.

| In | Line | Over |
|----|------|------|
| 0.8 | "Two plates. It used to be five." | 1 |
| 4.6 | "The rooms upstairs stopped being bedrooms years ago." | 2 |
| 8.0 | "You raised a family here. Nothing takes that away." | 3 |
| 11.4 | "But you're still mowing a yard for people who moved out." | 4 |
| 15.0 | "Still climbing stairs you don't need." | 5 |
| 18.2 | "And homes on your street are selling for more than you'd think." | 6 |
| 22.4 | "Less house. Same life." | 7 |
| 25.8 | "Find out what yours is worth. {{AGENT_NAME}}, {{BROKERAGE}}." | 8 |

Full read for the TTS/booth:

> Two plates. It used to be five. The rooms upstairs stopped being bedrooms
> years ago. You raised a family here — nothing takes that away. But you're
> still mowing a yard for people who moved out. Still climbing stairs you
> don't need. And homes on your street are selling for more than you'd think.
> Less house. Same life. Find out what yours is worth. {{AGENT_NAME}},
> {{BROKERAGE}}.

**Direction:** Line 1 is flat, almost offhand — it is an observation, not a
sad line. Lines 4 and 5 carry a little dry humor; the homeowner should feel
recognized, not pitied. Line 7 is the warmest read in the spot. Land
{{PHONE}} clean and slightly slower than the rest.

## On-screen text

Three text moments total. CTV is watched from ten feet away, so nothing
smaller than 3vmax and nothing that needs to be read twice.

| In | Out | Element | Copy |
|----|-----|---------|------|
| 18.4 | 21.8 | Market-Stat | "Homes in {{FARM_AREA}} are selling in {{DAYS_ON_MARKET}} days" |
| 26.2 | 30.0 | Endcard-Headline | "What's your home worth?" |
| 26.8 | 30.0 | Endcard-Contact | {{AGENT_NAME}} · {{BROKERAGE}} · {{PHONE}} |
| 28.4 | 30.0 | Endcard-Legal | "Equal Housing Opportunity" |

The endcard holds for the full last 4.6 seconds over the walking-away shot —
no cut to a card. The phone number must be on screen for at least 3 seconds
to be usable, and it is.

## Sound

- **Bed:** Solo piano, single sustained figure, no drums until the tablet
  shot. Warm, not sentimental — a music-box arrangement will make this feel
  like a funeral, which is the failure mode of the whole concept.
- **Lift:** Light pad and a soft pulse enter on the cross-zoom at 17.6.
- **Out:** Fade to nothing over the last 2.0s under the contact read.
- **Diegetic (optional, low):** Two cups on a table in 1; a distant mower in
  4; footsteps on gravel in 8. Keep every effect at least 18dB under the VO.

## Shot prompts (AI-generated footage)

Photoreal, 4–5s each, 16:9, no on-screen text, no logos, no readable faces
in close-up where noted. Generate 3 takes of each and pick — shots 1, 6 and
8 are the ones that most often need re-rolls.

**1 · Couple at breakfast**
> Cinematic wide shot, morning. A couple in their sixties sit at one end of a
> long kitchen table with two plates and two coffee cups. Four empty chairs
> at the far end, in soft focus. Warm low-angle sunlight through a window,
> dust in the air. Neither of them speaks; one stirs coffee. Slow, almost
> imperceptible push in. Photorealistic, shallow depth of field, muted warm
> grade.

**2 · Empty bedroom**
> Static wide shot of a bare child's bedroom, now empty. Stripped mattress,
> nail holes in the paint where posters hung, one cardboard box in the
> corner. Late morning light falls across the floor in a long rectangle.
> Absolutely no people. Dust drifting in the light beam. Photorealistic,
> still camera, quiet.

**3 · Family photos on the wall**
> Slow lateral dolly along a staircase wall covered in framed family
> photographs, spanning decades — a wedding, small children, graduations.
> Faces soft and non-specific, slightly out of focus. Warm hallway lamp
> light. Photorealistic, shallow depth of field, gentle continuous motion,
> no people in frame.

**4 · Lawn being mowed**
> A man in his sixties pushes a mower across a large green suburban lawn,
> seen from a distance so he reads small against the size of the yard. Late
> afternoon, long shadows. He pauses briefly and wipes his forehead. The
> house behind him is clearly bigger than one person needs. Photorealistic,
> locked-off wide shot, slight heat haze.

**5 · Stairs**
> Low-angle shot looking up a carpeted staircase in an older two-story home.
> A woman's feet and hand on the railing enter frame at the bottom and begin
> to climb, slowly, one step at a time. The top of the stairs is dim.
> Photorealistic, natural window light from the landing, shallow depth of
> field, no face visible.

**6 · Hands scrolling homes on a tablet**
> Close-up over-the-shoulder of two pairs of older hands holding a tablet
> between them at a kitchen counter. The screen shows a scrolling grid of
> home listing photos, swiping upward. One finger stops on a smaller
> single-story house. Warm evening kitchen light, screen glow on their hands.
> Photorealistic, macro detail on the hands, no readable text on the screen.

**7 · Smaller home exterior**
> Establishing shot of a well-kept single-story home on a modest lot, golden
> hour. Small porch with two chairs, tidy front garden, no lawn to speak of.
> A car in the driveway. Slow drone rise revealing the compact, manageable
> scale of the property. Photorealistic, warm late light, no people, no signs.

**8 · Couple walking away from camera**
> A couple in their sixties walk away from camera up a short front path
> toward the door of a small single-story home, seen from behind. She takes
> his arm. Neither turns around. Golden hour backlight, long shadows toward
> camera. Slow push in behind them. Photorealistic, cinematic, unhurried —
> the walk should read as arrival, not departure.

**Casting continuity:** the same two people must appear in shots 1, 4, 5, 6
and 8. When generating, lock a reference image of the couple from shot 1 and
feed it into every other shot with people in it, or the spot falls apart.

The market stat is the only claim in the spot. It has to be true and sourced
from current MLS data for that farm area, and it should be refreshed
whenever the campaign is refreshed. If real numbers aren't available for the
area, drop the `Market-Stat` element rather than soften it — the spot plays
fine without it.

## Compliance

- "Equal Housing Opportunity" holds on the endcard for the last 1.6s.
- Brokerage name appears in the VO and on the endcard, per most state
  advertising rules requiring the licensed firm to be identified.
- No claim about what the viewer's home is worth — only an offer to find out.
- No targeting of the spot by age, family status, or any protected class,
  regardless of who the creative depicts. Geography and behavior only.
